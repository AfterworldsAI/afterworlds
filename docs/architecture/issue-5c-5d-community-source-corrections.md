# Community source corrections — CRD Issue 5c/5d clean restart

What was changed in the pinned community Markdown, why, and against which
official page. The authoritative machine-readable list is
`docs/sources/community/dnd-5e-srd-markdown/source_pin.json`, which carries
each edit's path, official pages, section, change and reason, together with
the upstream digest and the corrected digest for every file. This document
groups those edits into the families they belong to, records the sibling audit
that bounded them, and names what was deliberately left alone.

Coverage figures — what was compared, how much was quiet, and every remaining
difference — are in
`docs/architecture/issue-5c-5d-community-source-coverage.json`, regenerated by
`venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/regen_community_source_coverage.py`.

This is a qualification of the source: its own defects resolved on its own
evidence. The final source boundary was reviewed independently at `b70d016`
(`source-structure-final-review.md`), which is what moves the status from
provisional to qualified. A later consumer reading these bytes is a different
question and is not what qualifies them, so nothing here waits on the
replacement 5c ingestion. Nothing here is a semantic acceptance either: what
the rules mean is decided by the Rules Package, not by this file.

## The corrections

Thirty-five edits across seven files: eight of them Owner corrections (family
L and the Veteran Warrior edit in family N), and one an official-source-backed
correction proposed under an Owner-requested lookup (family N). Upstream digests are preserved in the
manifest beside the corrected ones, so every edit is visible as a transition
rather than as a silently rewritten expectation.

### A. A body row wider than the table's header — 2 edits

`classes.md`, official pp.31 and 41. The Bard and Druid feature tables carried
a sixteenth cell on each of their twenty level rows against a fifteen-column
header. The extra cell was empty and it was the last one, so the fifteen
printed values before it stood under the headings that name them; what was
wrong was the table's shape, not the association of any value. The edit drops
the trailing cell. This is the only family where no printed value was ever
mislabelled, and it is recorded that way because the shape check that found it
cannot tell a trailing empty cell from a shifted row, and the difference
matters to anyone reading this list to decide what the snapshot once said.

### B. A MOD/SAVE legend standing over the wrong columns — 3 entries, 5 grids

`spells.md` pp.109, 131, 166 (Animate Objects, Find Steed, Summon Dragon)
and p.136 (Giant Insect); `magic-items.md` p.222 (Ebony Fly's
Giant Fly). As converted, the legend sat at the left of the row with `colspan`
filler parked at the end, so `MOD` stood over the ability name and `SAVE` over
the score. Every number was correct and every character was present — the
labels above them named the wrong quantity. The edit moves each legend onto
the columns it labels.

### C. Merged, shifted or missing cells in a stat-block ability grid — 3 edits

`monsters-A-Z.md` p.319 (Ancient Red Dragon, Remorhaz) and p.342
(Will-o'-Wisp). The conversion merged `+10 +10` into one cell, repeated `DEX`
as a value, copied CHA's modifier into its save cell, and in the Will-o'-Wisp
row dropped the STR score entirely, leaving eleven values in twelve columns.
Scores and modifiers are unchanged from the PDF; the edits restore the cell
boundaries, and `STR 1`, which was absent from the snapshot, is restored.

### D. A paragraph split at a printed column break — 4 edits

`magic-items.md` p.206 (Spells Cast from Items) and p.222 (Goat of Terror);
`spells.md` p.120 (Control Weather) and p.149 (Mind Spike). The PDF prints one
sentence; the conversion broke it into two paragraphs where the printed column
ends. The edits rejoin them.

### E. A table row lost under a header repeated at a page break — 1 edit

`magic-items.md`, official pp.246–247, Staff of the Magi. The Spell/Charge
Cost table runs across the page break and the official page reprints its
`Spell | Charge Cost` header at the top of p.247. The conversion dropped the
first body row under that repeated header, so `Mage Hand | 0` — one of the
staff's spells — was absent from the snapshot entirely. The edit restores the
row between `Lightning Bolt (level 7 version) | 7` and `Passwall | 5`.

This one is worth naming as an instrument result as well as a correction. The
page's token multiset did not report it: the header's words were present
either way, and the row's numbers reappear elsewhere in the same table. It was
found by reading each page's numeric tokens **in order**, where a repeated
header and a lost row do not look alike. That check is now
`community_tokens.number_order`, with a planted-defect control test.

### F. Wording the official page does not print — 1 edit

`spells.md` p.168, Telekinesis > Object. The pinned PDF ends this sentence
mid-clause at the foot of the left column, immediately before the Telepathic
Bond heading. The snapshot carried a continuation that appears on none of the
364 pages under either extractor. The edit truncates the sentence where the
PDF truncates it, and the removed wording is quoted verbatim in the manifest:

> ` opening a door or a container, stowing or retrieving an item from an open
> container, or pouring the contents from a vial.`

This is the one correction where the official source itself looks defective.
The page was rendered from the pinned PDF and read directly, and then rendered
and read again independently in review: the PDF truncates the sentence there.
Matching the PDF is what qualifying a source against it means, so the edit
stands at this stage on the evidence and needs no Owner opinion to proceed. It
is kept in its own family as an anomaly note about the official text — not as
a claim that the truncated sentence is correct, and not as an instruction to
any later runtime, which reads the Rules Package rather than this file. The
removed wording is quoted verbatim above and in the manifest, so reverting it
is a one-step edit of a quoted string if the official text is ever revised.

### G. A stat block emitted at its owning entry's depth — 6 edits

`spells.md` pp.109 (Animated Object), 131 (Otherworldly Steed), 136 (Giant
Insect) and 166 (Draconic Spirit); `magic-items.md` pp.222 (Giant Fly) and 232
(Avatar of Death). The official document sets these blocks inside the entry
that summons them — a spell or a magic item — but the conversion emitted each
one as a heading at the entry's own depth or above. In Markdown a heading
closes every open section at its depth or above, so each block ended the entry
holding it and captured every entry that followed, as far as the next heading
at that depth. `## Draconic Spirit` took Sunbeam through Zone of Truth, the
last spells in the file; `## Giant Insect` took Glibness onward; `## Otherworldly
Steed` took Find the Path onward; `### Actions` under a `####` magic item took
Necklace of Adaptation and every remaining item in `magic-items.md` into the
Avatar of Death's action list.

The edits set each block one level below the entry that summons it, and each
block's own `Traits` / `Actions` / `Bonus Actions` one level below the block —
the nesting the source already uses in `animals.md` and `monsters-A-Z.md`,
where a creature's labels sit exactly one level under the creature. No new
markup, no schema, and no prose changed.

One of the six needed more than a depth change. On official p.222 the Giant
Fly is set beside the Figurine of Wondrous Power entry rather than after it,
and the figurine variants from Golden Lions to Silver Raven are the entry's
own text, printed past the block. Markdown has no such inset, so nesting the
block where it stood would have left those variants inside the fly. The block
is therefore carried unchanged to the end of the Figurine entry, after Silver
Raven and before Flame Tongue. Every line of prose is preserved and every line
now resolves to the entry the official page gives it to. This is the one edit
in the family that changes the order of the source rather than only its
heading marks, and it is the reason official p.222 now appears among the pages
whose numeric tokens read in a different order (see below).

This family is why title presence in the official contents is reported as
title presence and nothing more. The contents list sections; it says nothing
about the depth of a heading or what that heading owns, and both comparison
passes are blind to heading marks by construction — a token bag and a sequence
of numbers do not contain `#`. Ownership needed its own pass, and now has one.

### H. A glossary entry emitted at the wrong rank — 1 edit

`rules-glossary.md` p.189 (Swim Speed), against official pp.189–191. The same
defect as family G — a heading emitted at a rank that makes it own its siblings
— in the one place family G could not look. The glossary is a single flat run of
155 entries under `## Rules Definitions`, and `### Swim Speed` was the sole `h3`
in it. Owning no `h4`, it closed the run instead: the thirteen entries from
Target to Weapon Attack, and the 47 units they hold, became its descendants.

The official pages print them as peers. Swim Speed and Swimming are on p.189,
Target and Telepathy on p.190, Unconscious and Weapon Attack on p.191, each a
glossary entry of the same rank as the 154 others. The edit is one byte —
`### Swim Speed` becomes `#### Swim Speed` — and it changes no prose, no
markup and no order. After it the entry run is 155 headings at one rank, every
one of which resolves to `Rules Glossary > Rules Definitions`.

**Why family G's pass could not see this.** That pass is organised around stat
blocks: it finds each block by its own `**AC**` and `**HP**` and reports the
depth of the block against the depth of the entry it resolves to. The glossary
embeds no stat block, so the pass had nothing to report there. The defect family
is heading-rank ownership, but the instrument was a stat-block instrument, and an
entry-run rank outlier is outside what it can admit. The new scan is organised
around the *run* instead, which is why it would fail on a rank outlier anywhere
in the glossary rather than only on this one.

This edit post-dates the independent source-boundary review at `b70d016` and
awaits review with the rest of this increment. It was found by a consumer of
these bytes rather than by the qualification passes, which is the honest record
of how it surfaced; what qualifies the correction is still the official pages,
read above.

### I. Italic group labels flattened into data rows — 2 edits

`equipment.md`, against official pp.100–102. The pages set a group label in
italic with its members indented under it; the conversion kept every character
but carried the label as an ordinary row.

* **Tack, Harness, and Drawn Vehicles** (p.100). `Saddle | | ` was a data row,
  so Saddle read as an item with no weight and no cost, and Exotic, Military
  and Riding as unqualified items. It is now `<th colspan="3"><em>Saddle</em></th>`.
* **Food, Drink, and Lodging** (pp.101–102). The pages set one `Item | Cost`
  list in paired sub-columns that snake column-major from p.101's right
  page-column to p.102's left. Read row-major as four columns, the conversion
  paired unrelated entries on one row (`Ale (mug) 4 CP | Comfortable 8 SP`),
  promoted two group labels into a second header row and gave the third
  (`Wine (bottle)`) no price, so the Comfortable, Wealthy and Aristocratic inn
  and meal prices read swapped. It is now a two-column list in page order with
  *Inn Stay per Day*, *Meal* and *Wine (bottle)* as group rows.

Both follow the convention the file's own Weapons and Armor tables already use.
Neither comparison pass could see this: the token multiset is unchanged, every
row has the header's width, and a column-major table read row-major has the same
ordered-number signature as a two-column page. That is why pp.101–102 still
appear in the number-order list below, now for this reason rather than reading
order. The residue was bounded by an italic-run scan of pp.89–103, which reports
exactly these four labels and none elsewhere in the Equipment chapter.

## Reading order across files

The corpus reads its files in the order `CORPUS_FILES`
(`src/afterworlds/ingestion/corpus/source_units.py`) gives them, and each
leaf's `occurrence_index` follows it. That order is what a direct read orders
passages by. The official document prints the general Monsters rules
(pp.254–257) before Monsters A–Z (p.258 onward), so `monsters.md` precedes
`monsters-A-Z.md`. No byte of either file changes for this. The other files
stay in the alphabetical order `CONTENT_FILES` gives them, which is not the
official chapter order (for example, `animals.md` comes first). Reordering
them is deferred: it is outside this bounded Monsters correction, not an
undecided Owner rule, and nothing here corrects it.

## What remains different, and why none of it is a correction

Seventy-two of the 360 body pages still show a token difference. Each was read.
All but one fall into five families, and none of them changes what the source
says. The one that does is official p.340, where the snapshot carries the
official-source-backed INT save `−2` against the page's `2` (family N).

1. **A table header the PDF reprints at a page break** — the snapshot carries
   it once. `Spell | School | Special` on nine spell-list pages, `1d100` on
   five roll tables, the item, CR/XP, rarity and vehicle tables, and the
   Food list's `Item | Cost` on p.102. The row
   under such a header is exactly what family E was, so every instance was
   checked by the ordered-number pass rather than assumed.
2. **A styled initial capital split into two tokens by the extractor** —
   `C`+`on` for `Con`, `D`+`ex`, `C`+`ha`, `W`+`is`, and the same in
   `short`+`sword`, `meta`+`magic`, `advan`+`tage`, `cast`+`ing`. The
   difference is in the PDF's rendering, not in either text.
3. **A spanning header flattened into repeated cells** — official p.202 prints
   `— Encounter Difficulty —` once across three columns; the conversion gives
   each of the three columns its own `Encounter Difficulty Low/Moderate/High`
   header. Lossless, and the column association is if anything clearer.
4. **Punctuation the two formats set differently** — the period ending a
   run-in heading (pp.89, 90, 92), quotation marks around a cross-reference
   (pp.7, 31, 49, 204, 225, 250), an em-dash legend decoration, a fraction
   slash (pp.96, 183), one comma (p.156).
5. **A block attributed to the neighbouring page's span** — pp.14/15,
   275/277, 293/295. The text is present, on the adjacent page of the same
   file; p.15's `action` / `a reaction` was read directly against
   `playing-the-game.md` and matches the PDF.

Sixty-four body pages differ in the order of their numeric tokens. All were
read. Every one is a two-column page whose reading order differs from the
snapshot's single column, with four exceptions. Official p.340 carries the
family N official-source-backed correction, so one number differs. Official pp.101–102 hold the
Food list that became correction I: the corrected file carries it in the order
the pages print it, and the extraction still reads across its paired
sub-columns. The Staff of the Magi table became correction E. Official p.222 joined the list with family G: the page
sets the Giant Fly beside the Figurine entry and the snapshot now carries the
block after it, so the same numbers are read in a different order. Its token
multiset is unchanged and the page stays quiet in the token pass, which is
what distinguishes a move from a loss.
