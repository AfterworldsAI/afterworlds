## Goal

Provide the minimum human-operable workflows needed to investigate access and service problems, perform authorized remediation, fulfill Story and account-wide export/deletion, and identify operational anomalies before responsible v1 launch.

Support must be able to explain what happened from durable, ordered records. A visible success must correspond to a committed effect; an incomplete operation must remain visibly incomplete.

Completion of CRD Issue 22 is separate from payment integration, legal-policy approval and the Owner's public-launch decision.

## Background and Architectural Position

The Construction Readiness Document's CRD Issue 22 entry, Items 5, 8 and 10–17, and Design §8 establish this scope.

Existing owners remain authoritative:

- **CRD Issue 13 / ADR-013:** runtime entitlements, credit arithmetic, access enforcement and settlement. `receive_entitlement_event` is the approved mutation path. Its append-only entitlement events are the accounting authority; CRD Issue 22 must not create a replacement ledger.
- **CRD Issues 14a/14b:** CredentialStore, credential metadata, readiness, provider routing, capability and refusal/fallback behavior. Support consumes safe views through those owners.
- **CRD Issues 3, 4 and 6 / ADR-0006:** narrative persistence, Story Bible and Rolling Summary. Retained history and transaction guarantees cannot be removed by an administrative shortcut.
- **CRD Issue 18 / ADR-018 D11:** Retrieval Memory deletion. Story deletion must remove story-filtered vector chunks and verify zero remain.
- **CRD Issues 19/20 ([#124](https://github.com/AfterworldsAI/afterworlds/issues/124), [#199](https://github.com/AfterworldsAI/afterworlds/issues/199)):** local-first app/API integration and authoritative billing/BYOK views. The browser presents and collects; services decide.
- **CRD Issue 23 / ADR-020:** payment, trial/subscription lifecycle, commercial entitlement changes, webhook idempotency and reconciliation. CRD Issue 23 emits operational events consumable here; this issue does not invent its future payment payloads.

The current implementation already has entitlement events with deterministic replay order, a reason-bearing manual credit-adjustment primitive, safe account/readiness views and a story-memory deletion helper. It lacks the complete support/request workflows. The current app has one server-provisioned local Sojourner identity, no staff identity and no cross-customer support authority. On 2026-10-17 the Owner confirmed remote-only production access for the subscription product, with local access possible initially for testing, and clarified that BYOK users need accounts for Cloud Services only. The subscription direction supersedes a local-only subscription launch assumption; the separate local BYOK path remains. Reconcile the enabling remote architecture explicitly with the earlier local-first shell contract without imposing cloud-account requirements on local BYOK use.

## Required Behavior

### 1. Authority and safe support lookup

Expose remote account operational state only within the accepted staff/target boundary. Enforce it in backend services and API dependencies; hiding controls is insufficient. Derive the remote actor from the trusted authenticated server context and authorize each selected target/action. Ordinary remote Sojourner requests are scoped to their authenticated owner; a submitted target ID is never proof of ownership. Reject forged identity/role fields, unauthorized subjects and unsupported targets without disclosing their data. Prove horizontal account isolation and denial of user-to-staff privilege escalation. Local BYOK data operations use the local owner boundary and cannot confer remote staff/account authority. Initially the Owner's authenticated administrator may target any hosted account; ordinary users remain limited to their own account. The administrator role must remain distinct even when the Owner also uses an ordinary Sojourner session.

Display authoritative included, top-up and total credits at existing precision; hosted plan/availability and unavailable reason; BYOK license status; and Cloud Services status, including effective lapse/expiry. Display credential readiness only from its owning source: local BYOK credential presence/readiness is not established by a remote account or license. A remote support view must identify unavailable local readiness rather than infer it, retrieve keys or silently introduce device telemetry/access. Distinguish unavailable reads from absent state, zero, negative balances and retained balances on inactive access.

Use existing owning-service reads. Lookup must not activate plans, repair projections, mutate credentials, contact providers or call a payment service. Surface only allowlisted operational metadata, not raw ORM payloads, story prose or hidden RPG state.

### 2. Ordered and reconstructable history

Present entitlement history from the existing event source, preserving stable event IDs and ascending `global_sequence` as replay order. Created/effective timestamps are not an alternative ordering rule. State what the displayed history covers; pagination must preserve stable order without silent omissions or duplicates.

Support and request records must identify their schema version, stable operation/request and event references, subject scope, trusted actor/provenance, action or transition, reason where required, outcome and recorded time. Define deterministic order within each event stream. Correlate streams by durable references; do not imply unrelated sequence counters establish a universal causal order.

Operational transitions are append-safe and reconstructable after restart. Corrections append a superseding event linked to the original; normal service paths cannot edit or delete prior history. Do not duplicate entitlement events as a second accounting authority. Terminal deletion and approved retention expiry use only the separately governed D2 lifecycle exception; do not treat ordinary append-safety as blanket indefinite retention of personal or creative content.

Replay/inspection failures, unsupported event versions and detected projection mismatches remain explicit. A read must not silently rebuild or overwrite the entitlement projection. If remediation is needed, it follows an authorized, logged operation.

### 3. Controlled manual remediation

For each action accepted under D3, collect a nonblank operator reason and show its target and proposed effects before explicit execution. For credit adjustments, show the signed included/top-up deltas and authoritative current balances, retain existing decimal validation and expose stale/conflicting state truthfully.

Derive `adjusted_by` from D1's trusted actor context. Invoke `receive_entitlement_event` with the matching typed payload and a stable idempotency key for the logical action. Preserve existing preconditions, including refusal of credit mutation where hosted access was never activated. Do not activate a plan to make an adjustment succeed.

Identical retry/repeated submission of the same action must refer to the same committed result and apply the delta once. Reusing its identity with different target or payload must conflict. Distinct authorized concurrent actions must not overwrite one another. An ambiguous transport result requires a status/result read before another effect is attempted; no blind automatic mutation retry from the browser.

The successful entitlement event itself may supply the durable reason/actor/effect record. If additional support records are needed, account for the entitlement service's transaction ownership and internal commit: a crash or failed follow-up write cannot erase or mislabel an applied adjustment. Recover by the original operation/event identity; do not apply a compensating adjustment silently or create a shadow ledger.

Show success only after durable result confirmation, including the event reference and refreshed authoritative state. Rejection, failure and uncertain outcomes must remain distinguishable. Audit failure before an effect must prevent that effect; an effect already committed must remain discoverable even if the response or ancillary write fails.

### 4. Data-request lifecycle

Provide Sojourner-facing request/status paths for both Story and account-wide export/deletion, and the administrator review/execution path. Scope remote requests to the authenticated subscription/Cloud Services account; local BYOK export/deletion uses the local owner boundary without requiring a cloud account. Persist request kind, owning environment, stable subject/scope, requester provenance, creation time and request ID. Account-wide scope enumerates all account-owned data before removal; it cannot depend on parent links after they are deleted. A remote request does not imply access to a user's device. Requesting deletion is distinct from authorizing and executing it. As sole administrator, the Owner may approve and execute the same request; record the two steps separately without requiring a second staff member.

Define typed lifecycle states covering requested, awaiting policy/authorization, approved, in progress, completed, failed and rejected. State transitions require the appropriate actor, append an ordered event, and use the expected current revision to reject stale or invalid transitions. An explicit retry creates a traceable new attempt on the same request; it does not rewrite failure history. Record the original receipt time, verification/confirmation times, policy version and applicable phase deadlines. Export's service clock starts with the verified request; deletion's service clock starts when identity/ownership verification and explicit scope confirmation are satisfied. Waiting for administrator approval, retries, restarts, copied backups and new attempt IDs do not restart those clocks. Preserve any applicable legal response clock from its legally required starting point; do not silently replace it with the later service-clock start. Missing verification or a genuine preservation requirement must have a bounded, source-backed disposition and visible status, not an indefinite completed or paused request.

Both accepted Story and account-wide requests must have working fulfillment paths before this issue can pass. Missing policy, unavailable stores or dependencies may block an individual attempt visibly, but cannot convert either required scope into a permanently tracked-only capability. Truly unsupported/out-of-scope requests may be rejected with a safe reason. A manual status edit is not fulfillment. Reload/restart recovers remaining work; interrupted or partially completed operations cannot silently become completed.

### 5. Export

Produce usable, portable Story and account-wide artifacts from a consistent persisted view. Account export enumerates all account-owned Stories/content and permitted account/settings/operational data, not only the current Story. Include a versioned manifest identifying subject, request scope, included classes, snapshot/revision boundaries, per-class/per-Story counts and deliberate exclusions. Human-readable transcripts and structured story/state data must agree with those boundaries; account export must not mix another account's data or silently omit a Story because of pagination or an orphaned parent link. Document supported reading/portability; import/restore is not added by this issue.

Apply existing player-visible projections to user-facing exports. Do not leak hidden rolls/modifiers, internal adjudication or unpublished shared Rules Package source by serializing raw database rows. Do not include raw BYOK keys, credential-store contents, tokens, unsafe exception payloads or unrelated subjects' records. Account export includes the approved account/settings and minimized operational-record view under D2; the manifest identifies excluded internal/security fields. Do not export the whole SQLite file or use access to the server database as a substitute for ownership and visibility filtering.

Deliver the artifact only to the authorized requester/recipient through the appropriate authenticated remote or local-owner boundary. Local BYOK export does not require upload to the server or cloud-account sign-in. Completion requires a successfully produced, accessible artifact and recorded manifest/result reference. Failed generation or delivery leaves a failed/incomplete request and no misleading success or broken download. Produce and make the hosted artifact accessible within 7 calendar days of the verified request; retain it for 7 days from availability, then delete the hosted artifact and revoke its download access. Delete failed/partial staging artifacts within 24 hours of failure/abandonment. Record availability/expiry and cleanup evidence. These timers govern service-managed copies; user-downloaded files and deliberately saved local BYOK exports remain under the local owner's control. Do not preserve export bodies in long-lived backups to bypass expiry; any residual managed copy remains inventoried and subject to the backup/deletion rules. Offer the requested export and provide the completion/retention receipt through an authorized delivery/status path before account closure would strand the requester. Account closure must not make an in-progress deletion status or final receipt inaccessible; do not expose receipts by unauthenticated, guessable request IDs.

Owned-content read/export/download must remain available despite hosted-credit exhaustion or Cloud Services lapse. Export does not require a provider call or create a new billing/access gate.

### 6. Deletion and retained data

Before execution, present Story versus account-wide scope, the data to be removed, the minimal retained-record categories and reasons, and the active-data/backup completion limits. Require explicit confirmation against the displayed scope/revision. Remove in-scope live data within 7 calendar days of the verified, confirmed request; fence new writes and revoke applicable sessions when execution starts. There is no added undelete/grace period. Any overdue or blocked effect remains visible and cannot be relabelled as completed. Closing an account, cancelling a commercial subscription and erasing its data are distinct effects; coordinate required subscription/renewal closure and pending commercial events through CRD Issue 23's owning contracts before severing references needed to finish them. No refund/payment semantics are invented here, and an unavailable commercial dependency cannot be reported as completed account closure.

Build a durable ownership inventory before removing parent links: Story/Arc/Chapter/Node/Turn data; Story Bible including historical canon values; Rolling Summaries; setup/session state; account-owned Character Sheets and bindings; RPG audit/action payloads; retrieval markers/chunks; account/profile/settings/authentication/session data where applicable; generated exports/staging files; account-scoped caches/replicas; and backups. Story deletion affects its owned content; separately owned sheets still used elsewhere require their declared disposition. Account deletion covers every account-owned Story and hosted sheet, not shared Rules Package authority or other accounts. Record each class as removed, minimally retained under the approved schedule, shared/unaffected, pending or failed; no unclassified data may silently become a retained exception.

Do not use bare Story CRUD as proof of deletion: `Turn.node_id` currently becomes null when its Node is removed. Explicitly remove in-scope Turn prose, prior narrative/canon values and derived copies under the accepted lifecycle, preserving durable deletion scope before detachment. Immutable RPG-audit references and historical no-delete contracts require the reviewed D2 exception and supported owning-service/migration paths. Do not disable triggers ad hoc, break foreign keys, silently redact source history or retain full content-bearing rows solely to avoid reconciling those contracts.

Use owning services for effects. Coordinate Story deletion with active turns, setup/confirmation operations, RPG resumes, persistent turn leases and retrieval ingestion/reindex. Account deletion also fences new account-owned writes and coordinates relevant background work and delayed external events. Prevent new work, webhook/redelivery handling, backfill or restore from recreating deleted content or accounts. Preserve unrelated accounts' availability. Under the accepted terminal-disposal amendment, a confirmed deletion fences resume/submission and removes the deleted Story's pending/sequence/event state; it produces no gameplay outcome, rewind, reroll, credit reversal or refund. Continuing-Story cancellation/expiry remains unresolved under its existing owner. An unfenceable in-flight operation is a visible operational failure to resolve within the approved deadline, not an indefinite gameplay-policy hold or false completion.

Delete all matching story-memory chunks through the ADR-018 path and verify zero remain for every Story in the request. Account-wide verification enumerates all account-owned Stories from the durable inventory, including content whose parent references are about to disappear. A missing collection may establish zero through the existing delete-only path; an unavailable/failed Chroma read cannot. Leave shared Rules Package collections and other accounts/Stories intact. Later backfill/reindex must not resurrect deleted material from retained rows.

SQLite, ChromaDB, account/authentication stores, files and backups do not share one atomic transaction. Persist per-store/per-attempt progress, retry remaining steps safely, and distinguish removed, retained, pending and unverified data. Once an irreversible step succeeds, do not claim a global rollback. Revoke/close remote account sessions and access through the accepted identity owner while preserving an authorized status/receipt path. A terminal account identifier cannot be silently reused to recreate deleted data. Completion requires the accepted live-store postconditions, retained-record receipt and backup obligations; active-data deletion alone is not final completion while required backup cleanup remains pending.

#### Retained-record policy

Use an explicit per-class allowlist; being an append-only table is not a retention justification. The accepted boundaries are:

| Data class | Deletion disposition | Permitted retained content |
|---|---|---|
| Story content and derived state | Remove in-scope transcripts, Story Bible current/history values, summaries, session/setup content, account-owned sheets, retrieval copies and content-bearing diagnostic payloads through the reviewed lifecycle exception. | No retained narrative prose, canon history, full prompt/provider output or full RPG snapshots merely for generic support convenience. |
| Remote account/profile/authentication data | Close account access and remove in-scope profile/settings/session data through its owner. | Only necessary minimized subject references in the approved operational categories; no blanket copy of the profile or authentication secrets. |
| Accounting/entitlement and perpetual-rights evidence | Preserve only the approved minimized record needed to reconstruct recorded credit/access effects and preserve applicable perpetual BYOK rights. | Stable event/subject references, ordering, type/time, necessary credit/state effects and non-secret provenance/operational reason. Enumerate exact retained fields and purpose; do not retain unrelated creative/profile content. |
| Administrator-action and data-request evidence | Preserve the approved minimized operational receipt, with an explicit retention/review end condition. | Trusted administrator/requester reference, action/scope, authorization, time, bounded operational reason, outcome/counts, retained categories and completion/expiry evidence. No artifact bodies, arbitrary raw payloads or secrets. |
| Deletion-suppression references | Keep the minimum needed to prevent restored backups, delayed jobs or external-event redelivery from resurrecting deleted subjects/content. | Minimal subject/Story/request references, deletion boundary/time and enforcement status; access-limited and governed by an explicit end condition. |
| Shared Rules Packages, other accounts' data and device-local BYOK data | Unaffected by remote account/Story deletion unless separately within a confirmed owned local request. | No new remote device access or cloud credential collection. |

The accepted linked field/store matrix is the field-level contract. Before affected implementation map actual providers/stores and necessary extra fields, identifying purpose, owning service/store, authorized readers, allowed fields, the approved duration or end condition below, expiry disposition and any specifically justified exception. Retention expiry must have an owned, auditable lifecycle path consistent with replay and license-rights contracts. A retained identifier is not automatically anonymous; do not make that claim. Additional retained content or longer periods require an explicit, source-backed disposition and authority, not a fallback to retaining the full source record.
