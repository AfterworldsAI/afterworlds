## Reference 34

PDF pages 205-206, Magic Item Values by Rarity, conditions the addition on an
incorporated item having a purchase cost in Equipment. +1 Plate Armor is
explicitly 5,500 GP; Sword of Wounding in Glaive form is 4,000 + 20 = 4,020 GP.
Instant Fortress is Rare, and the valuation rule establishes no Equipment
addition for its statuette or tower. The rarity guidance therefore yields
4,000 GP; it does not guarantee availability or an actual sale price.
Consumables and Spell Scrolls retain their source-specific adjustments.

missing-base-price boundary and need for an Owner Decision. This settles the
interpretation, not an exact destination mapping. Full priced-Equipment coverage

and recording. Source: qualified magic-items.md:177-224.

## Reference 36

PDF page 255 and qualified monsters.md:169-175 direct someone using retrievable
Gear to the ordinary Equipment rules, without the monster's special flourishes.
A bounded inventory of every explicit `**Gear**` entry in the qualified source
found 45 entries, all in monsters-A-Z.md, containing 39 distinct normalized item
names. Every name matches an Equipment table row, heading, or bold tool entry.
Quantity suffixes were removed; Daggers, Handaxes, Javelins, Light Hammers, and
Spears matched their singular Equipment names. Animals and the other source
files have no additional explicit Gear entries. Thieves' Tools is a bold tool
entry at equipment.md:760, not a missing item.

The earlier phrase "form plus its parent rule" meant the Wand row in Equipment's
Arcane Focuses table (equipment.md:1295-1297) together with the Arcane Focus
paragraph explaining its use (equipment.md:1259-1261). This is ordinary
table-and-prose coverage, not a semantic problem or new ownership requirement.
No missing-item example was found. Disposition: ordinary destination mapping
and consumer coverage under the existing contract; no new Owner Decision is
demonstrated.

The outside-Gear rule remains separate: the SRD supplies a general default that
such equipment is unusable when the monster is defeated. Absence from Gear does
not establish physical unretrievability and must not become an "unretrievable"
flag. For example, the Solar's Flying Sword (PDF p.326; monsters-A-Z.md:15792)
appears outside Gear, but its description does not prohibit collecting it.
Specific source text, including an adventure's explicit exception, can establish
whether and how such equipment becomes usable: the SRD's Exceptions Supersede
General Rules principle applies (playing-the-game.md:105-109). Preserving the
generic default does not imply an irrevocable prohibition on learning its use,
and following a specific exception requires no house-rule amendment. The GM can
also add gear and decide its recoverability/usability (monsters.md:181-185).
These matters do not block reference 36's ordinary Equipment mapping.
